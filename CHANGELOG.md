# Changelog

## v1.7.1 — WHEEL TO WHEEL (Nov 25, 2026)
- **Single-file racing with real battles** on every non-drafting track: cars run in the racing line, close up on the car ahead, pull out to pass (side by side only for a few seconds), then complete the pass or lift and tuck back in; the car being passed sometimes defends. Positions change on track instead of the field cruising two-wide.
- **Road, street and kart tracks:** passes are started mainly into the braking zones, then the field goes back to single file.
- **Drafting tracks keep pack racing:** Daytona, Talladega and Atlanta (the superspeedway type, now flagged `pack`) still run two- and three-wide drafting packs. Restarts stay double file everywhere; caution, pit and restart logic unchanged.
- **Balance kept:** the time a pass gains or costs is evened out afterwards and the pace spread is recalibrated, so lap times, finishing spreads and who runs up front stay close to v1.7.
- **Kart tracks get their own pit lane** (Orlando Kart, Ocala GP, GoPro, CMS Kart, New Castle, PittRace): a separate road branching off the track to a single straight row of full-size boxes (12 units per box for big fields, 16 for karts), placed in free space beside the track, clear of the track surface, curbs and grandstand. Fixes the shrunken cars and squeezed two-row lane with Truck / O'Reilly / Cup / IndyCar fields in Free Mode. Works in Classic and 3D.

## v1.7 — 3D Race View (Nov 25, 2026)
- **New setting: Race View — Classic / 3D** (Settings; Classic stays the default, older settings and saves carry over). In 3D only the track and the cars are drawn in 3D (three.js r170, loaded only when 3D is used); the leaderboard, pit-wall strip, radio calls, flags, buttons and every overlay stay exactly as they were.
- **Every track:** banked ovals (banking from the real track data) with SAFER walls, catch fences, aprons and grandstands; road courses and kart tracks as flat ribbons with red/white curbs on the bends and grass; street circuits between concrete walls and fences; pit road with the real pit boxes (two-row lanes included), pit walls and crew carts; the start/finish line.
- **Cars:** stock cars, trucks, open-wheel cars and karts in team colours with their numbers. The owner's cars wear a yellow (car 1) or cyan (car 2) marker — you are the team owner, never the driver.
- **Cameras:** TV cam (high broadcast angle, follows your selected car or the leader) and Chase, on the existing camera button (C on a keyboard).
- **Weather:** rain darkens the sky, adds rain streaks and gives the track a wet look.
- **3D Quality: Low / High** (Low = no shadows, pixel ratio 1; High = 1024 shadow map, pixel ratio up to 2). Drawn only while the race screen is visible.
- **Fallbacks:** no WebGL = the race runs in the Classic view (with a note); if the phone drops the 3D view mid-race, the rest of the race carries on in Classic.
- Includes the v1.6.2 pit box fix.

## v1.6.2 — Pit box fix (Nov 25, 2026)
- **Pit boxes never overlap or run into the turns.** The pit row on road, kart and street tracks is now fitted between the corners: it keeps its boxes if they fit, shifts along the straight, shrinks the boxes (never below a car), and switches to a **two-row pit lane** (boxes on both sides of the fast lane, the Detroit layout) when the straight is too short.
- **All 6 kart tracks** (Orlando, Ocala, GoPro, CMS Kart, New Castle, PittRace) had their 16 boxes folding over each other in the hairpin; they now get a straight two-row pit / grid lane.
- Big fields on short ovals (Phoenix, Richmond, Iowa, Dover, New Hampshire, Gateway, Nashville with 28-40 cars) get a two-row pit lane instead of tiny boxes; tight road courses with big fields (Sonoma, Watkins Glen, COTA, Barber, Mid-Ohio, Laguna Seca, Long Beach) keep their boxes off the corners.
- Curbs from another part of a tight track are no longer painted across pit road.
- The in-game version label now says V1.6.2 (it still said V1.6 after the 1.6.1 patch).

## v1.6 — Owner's Box (Nov 24, 2026)
*The business side of the team (charters, rivals, manufacturers) and race day (driver radio, pit crew, weather, driver drama).*
- **You are still the owner / manager, never the driver.**
- **New BUSINESS button on the hub** (bottom bar, next to Free Mode; B on a keyboard) with three tabs: Charters, Rivals and Manufacturer. A red dot counts rival offers and manufacturer offers waiting for an answer. The hub still fits on one screen at 844x390 and 740x360 (the 9 tiles are unchanged).
- **Cup charters:**
  - The Cup now has a 40-car field and a fixed 36 charters. A chartered car is guaranteed a starting spot and earns a **30% bigger share of the purse** (a CHARTER SHARE line on the results).
  - Open (non-chartered) cars race in on qualifying speed for the last 4 spots - the field carries a couple of extra open teams, so open cars can **fail to qualify**. A DNQ shows a clear DID NOT QUALIFY screen with the open-car qualifying list (IN / DNQ), a SIT OUT THE RACE button and the charter tip; the race runs without you, there is no purse or points, and salary and travel are still paid. If only one of two cars misses, the other races and the results show which car went home.
  - **The charter market** (Business > Charters): buy a charter from a rival owner, sell one, lease one in for the rest of the season, or lease yours out for cash. Up to 2 charters (one per car). Charters can be bought from the O'Reilly Series up (lease it out until your Cup debut). The market value drifts week to week and jumps between seasons; your own charter is worth more when your team runs well in the Cup points. Leases end with the season.
  - The affordable way into the Cup stays: the Cup buy-in gets you in as an open team, and leasing a charter costs a fraction of buying one.
  - Hired managers: the boss owns the team's charters, so a hired Cup team always starts.
- **Rival owners:** four made-up AI owners - Vance Kolby (Kolby Motorsports), Delia Marchetti (Marchetti Racing Group), Holt Brannigan (Brannigan-Hayes Racing) and Reggie Okafor (Okafor Performance), each with their own style.
  - They try to poach your drivers (mostly when a contract is in its last year, or the driver is running up front) and your crew chief. You get a RIVAL ALERT in the news, on the results screen and on the Business button.
  - **Counter** (a bidding war of 1-3 rounds: they may raise again) or **let go**. Morale decides how loyal a driver is when you match. A won counter sets the new salary and adds a year if the deal was ending. A driver with years left leaves at once and the rival pays a buyout; a final-year driver races out the season and then joins the rival. Unanswered offers expire after 2 races.
  - Rivals also bid for scouted prospects: when you sign one, a RIVAL BID box may ask you to outbid them (10% over their offer) or let the prospect go.
- **Crew chief:** every team now has a crew chief with a rating; a good one makes pit stops a little quicker. A raise won in a bidding war is paid every race. Lose yours and someone from the shop steps up (a bit weaker).
- **Manufacturer deals** (Business > Manufacturer): Chevrolet, Ford or Toyota on the NASCAR ladder; Honda or Chevrolet engines in IndyCar. The junior open-wheel series run spec engines (no deals), and so do karts.
  - Offers arrive with reputation and results: Customer, Supported or Factory deals with cash paid every race, an engine boost, and goals (top-15 / top-10 / top-5 finishes, a race win).
  - Goals are checked at season end: all met = a bigger renewal offer; half met = a smaller one; missed = the deal ends and that make sits out a season.
  - One manufacturer at a time; switching mid-deal costs money. Your make shows as a badge on the hub (on the Business button).
- **Charlotte** gets a longer, flatter frontstretch (closer to the real quad-oval) so its 40 Cup pit boxes stay roomy.
- **Race day - driver radio calls:** during a race your driver now and then radios a handling problem - "car's loose in", "tight off", "rears are going" or "vibration" - in a small radio bubble on the track view (it never stops the race).
  - At your next stop a **RADIO FIX** panel opens next to the pit menu: WEDGE, TRACK BAR or AIR PRESSURE for stock cars; FRONT WING, REAR WING or PRESSURES for open-wheel cars; or NO CHANGE (saves time). The crew chief's pick is marked. Keyboard: 7 / 8 / 9 / 0.
  - The right fix brings the pace back (and a touch more); the wrong fix or no fix and the car keeps getting slower until it is sorted. A vibration is also cured by a 4-tire stop.
  - Drivers with good feedback (tire management + consistency, plus personality) make clear calls; a vague call ("the back end feels funny") shows as ??? and the crew chief can guess wrong. A better crew chief guesses better. Qualifying trim brings more handling calls, race trim fewer.
- **Race day - pit crew + training** (Business > PIT CREW):
  - Hire a pit crew from Local Volunteers (free) to an Elite Crew - 5 levels, paid every race (a PIT CREW line on the results). Train the crew once between races; sharpness fades a little every race.
  - One **PIT CREW SPEED** number shows the whole picture so nothing is counted twice: base + pit center + crew chief (from pass A) + pit crew level + training, with the 4-tire stop time it means. The crew chief makes the calls and the radio fixes; the pit crew does the stop.
  - **Pit-stop tap game:** when your car stops in its box the race pauses for a quick tap game - one tap per wheel when the marker is on the green zone (a better crew gets a wider zone). Perfect / good taps shave time, misses add time and can cause a **loose-wheel penalty** (held on pit road). Big 48px+ TAP button, mouse click or SPACE. AUTO skips it and uses your crew level; a wheel you do not tap within 3 seconds is done on AUTO. Turn the game off on the qualifying screen (PIT STOP TAP GAME: ON/OFF); it is also off when caution calls are off and when you skip.
- **Race day - weather:**
  - Every race weekend has a **forecast** - sunny, cloudy or rain likely, with a rain chance - on the practice and qualifying screens and in the hub ticker. A weather icon sits in the race top bar (RAIN, WET, RED FLAG), and light rain is drawn on the track picture under the cars, so it never hides them.
  - **Ovals:** rain brings a caution, then a **RED FLAG** (the field is held). Before halfway the jet dryers do their job and the race restarts under caution; past halfway the race can be **called for rain** and the result is official (a RAIN-SHORTENED note on the results and in the news).
  - **Road and street courses** race in the rain: wets or slicks. Slicks on a wet track are slow and slide off; wets on a drying track are slow and wear out. Timing the switch matters. The crew chief radios when it is time ("track is drying - time for slicks?") and the pit fix panel gets WETS / SLICKS buttons (keys 5 / 6). AI teams make their own tire calls, some earlier, some later.
- **Race day - driver drama** (Business > DRAMA):
  - Drivers have personalities: **hothead, calm, showman, team player** (from their aggression). Hotheads hold grudges, showmen sell drama, team players give great feedback and calm down easily.
  - On-track contact with one of your cars can start a **feud**; a driver with a hot grudge can **wreck his rival on purpose** in a later race. An intentional wreck brings a **NASCAR fine and lost points** (race control in open-wheel); a rival who wrecks your driver gets fined and your driver wants payback.
  - Drama brings **fan hype** and a DRAMA MERCH BOOST every race (it fades).
  - It shows in the news ticker, on the results, and as an owner talk in Business > DRAMA (the BUSINESS dot counts them): **BACK HIM** (morale up, feud hotter), **FINE HIM** (morale down, money back, cools it) or **CALM DOWN** (cools the grudge - works best on calm drivers and team players). Morale feeds the pass-A loyalty when a rival tries to poach him. Unanswered talks expire after 2 races (a small morale hit).
- **Saves:** v1.0 to v1.5 saves (and earlier v1.6 saves) load. They get a crew chief and the business layer with no deals and no offers; a Cup team from an older save keeps racing with a charter (one per car) and keeps its current field until the next season. Every old save starts race day with a volunteer pit crew, no training, no feuds or talks, and a personality for each driver.

## v1.3 — Two Roads (Nov 23, 2026)
- **You are still the owner / manager, never the driver.**
- **Careers start in karting:** a cheap kart team ($6K as an owner, or a kart team hires you) in a regional kart series at real US kart tracks: Orlando Kart Center (the season opener), Ocala Gran Prix, GoPro Motorplex, New Castle Motorsports Park, the Charlotte Motor Speedway kart track and PittRace. Karts have their own pixel art, 20-lap sprints, no stages and young drivers (ages 12+). Kart layouts are simplified pixel versions, not surveyed maps.
- **Pick your path after the kart season:** NASCAR (ARCA > Trucks > O'Reilly > Cup) or the Road to IndyCar (USF Juniors > USF2000 > USF Pro 2000 > Indy NXT > IndyCar). Owners sell the kart and local backers top the bank up to at least $15K for the first big car; kart R&D gives that car a small edge. Hired managers get one NASCAR offer and one Indy offer. You can also run another kart season.
- **Road to IndyCar:** open-wheel cars, IndyCar-style points (+1 pole, +1 led a lap, +2 most laps led), no stages, and real-style schedules on 15 new real tracks (St. Petersburg and Long Beach streets, Barber, the Indianapolis road course, Detroit, Road America, Mid-Ohio, Laguna Seca, Toronto, Portland, NOLA, Lucas Oil, Gateway / WWT Raceway, Milwaukee, Nashville) plus Iowa, Watkins Glen, Sonoma and the Indianapolis oval. IndyCar opens at St. Pete, runs the Indy 500 in race 5 (longer race, bonus purse, its own trophy) and ends at Nashville with double points.
- **Free Mode:** a FREE MODE button on the hub (Q on a keyboard). Pick any series, any track (the series' own or all 45) and any team, then race a one-off weekend. Works without a career and never touches the career save; leaving at any point puts your career back exactly as it was.
- **Tracks look more like the real thing:** grandstands where the real ones are (Daytona's huge frontstretch, Bristol's bowl all the way around, Martinsville's front and back stands, Talladega's long frontstretch, Indy's turn stands), infield garages, Daytona's and Talladega's infield lakes, and city blocks with concrete walls on street courses.
- **Real pit roads:** cars peel off before the start/finish at a yellow commitment line, every car has its own evenly spaced pit box along a long pit road (on short tracks it wraps into the turns, like Bristol and Martinsville; the pole sitter gets the box nearest pit exit), stopped cars pull into their box, and the exit blends back in after turn 1-2.
- **Merch and team events:** merch sales every race (rep, results and the new Fan Shop), and random team events (about 30% of races): sponsor bonus checks, viral moments, fan crowdfunds, local TV features, contingency awards, hauler breakdowns, series fines, shop floods, tire price hikes, an engineer quitting, a driver with the flu. Bad events are rarer while you are in the red.
- **Tech inspection:** qualifying trim, a car far above the field and brand-new R&D parts raise the risk (shown on the practice setup buttons). Failures cost a fine, a fine plus 10-25 points, or (rarely) a disqualification to last place.
- **Playoffs with eliminations:** Cup 16 (after R4) > 12 (R6) > 8 (R8) > Championship 4 (R9) with win-and-advance; O'Reilly 12 > 8 > 4 and Trucks 10 > 8 > 4. IndyCar and Indy NXT end with a double-points finale. A save made mid-season before v1.3 finishes that season on the old format.
- **A car for every series:** kart, ARCA, Truck, O'Reilly, Cup, USF (junior open-wheel), USF Pro, Indy NXT and IndyCar (with aeroscreen), in the race view, garage, podium and menus.
- **Contract extensions:** offer +1 to +3 years as a Lowball, Fair or Generous deal (max 5 years). Morale, age and team rep decide if they sign; a yes lifts morale, a no lowers it and you wait a race to ask again. Drivers in their final year are flagged.
- **More upgrades:** facilities go to level 8; new Wind Tunnel (aero), Chassis Rig (chassis) and Fan Shop (merch); R&D gets Major Project (Shop LV 3) and Moonshot (Shop LV 6) tiers.
- **Names:** a new game starts with EMPTY owner and team name boxes (placeholder text only) and you must type both. A bad-word blocker checks every name you type: owner name (new game, hired manager and profile edits), team name and driver names. You can now rename your drivers. Generated names are untouched.
- **ARCA is a little friendlier:** slightly weaker top teams, bigger purses, cheaper crash repairs, top 6 (or 2 wins) earns the call-up, and the Trucks buy-in drops to $150K. In bot tests most teams now move up after their 2nd ARCA season instead of the 3rd or 4th.
- **Saves:** v1.0, v1.1, v1.2 and v1.2.1 saves all load and stay on the NASCAR path; they get the new facilities at level 1. Players with a save see a "what's new" pop-up once.

- **Saves:** v1.0 and v1.1 saves load as they are. Old made-up tracks map to the closest real track (Bayshore > Daytona, Talla Pines > Talladega, Thunder Valley > Bristol, Paperclip > Martinsville, Hickory Bowl > Richmond, Riverbend > Iowa, Lone Star > Texas, Carolina > Charlotte, Desert Sun > Las Vegas, Heartland > Kansas, Blue Ridge > Watkins Glen, Lakeside > Sonoma) in the schedule, results, trophies and news.

## v1.1 — Two Cars & Talent (Nov 22, 2026)
- **Second car:** buy a 2nd chassis in the Garage once the team has 20+ reputation and the cash ($45K in ARCA, more in higher series). It has its own driver, its own primary sponsor, its own prize money, and per-race travel & crew costs (about $6K/race in ARCA) plus driver pay and repairs. You can sell it back for 40%.
- **Both team cars race:** both are highlighted on track (yellow arrow = car 1, cyan = car 2) and in the leaderboard. Each car gets its own pit-wall calls (tap the car tabs on race day), and under caution you're asked for each car in turn.
- **Owner points:** Standings has a new Owners tab that adds both cars together.
- **Hired Manager:** bigger teams in Trucks and up can run two cars.
- **Driver stats:** Speed, Consistency, Racecraft, Tire Mgmt, Fuel Saving, Pit Road/Restarts and Aggression, all used by the race sim. Drivers also show Age, Potential stars, Morale, Salary and Contract on pixel stat cards. Young drivers grow toward their potential; veterans slow down.
- **Scouting:** send a Local, Regional or National scout to Karting, Late Models, Dirt Ovals or Legends Cars. Prospects show up as stat ranges and potential-star ranges that narrow each time you scout them again. A higher Simulator level sharpens reports. Prospects sign with other teams if you wait too long.
- **Development drivers:** sign up to 2 prospects to grow on the side, then swap them into a car.
- **Podium celebration:** after every race, the top 3 stand on an animated pixel podium with confetti, champagne and a trophy, plus extra fireworks when your car is up there. A bigger night-sky version crowns the season champion. Tap anywhere or press Results to skip.
- **Trophy cabinet:** a new Trophies tile on the hub with shelves for race wins (per track), podiums (per track), championships (per series), Cup playoffs (berth, Championship 4, Cup champion), promotions, and milestones (first start, first top-10, first win, pole, stage win, first sponsor, first prospect, two-car team, one-two finish, 10 and 25 wins, millionaire). Locked trophies show as silhouettes with how to earn them. Tap any trophy for details.
- **Update log:** tap the V1.1 label on the hub (or Profile > Update Log). "What's new" shows once for existing players.
- **Saves:** v1.0 saves load automatically. Every driver gets the new stats, and trophies are rebuilt from the current season's results and your season history.
